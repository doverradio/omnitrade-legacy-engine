# Purpose

The Asset Correlation Model measures how financial assets move relative to one another.

Its purpose is to help OmniTrade understand whether multiple assets represent genuinely independent opportunities or merely different expressions of the same underlying market movement.

Understanding correlation improves:

- portfolio diversification
- capital allocation
- opportunity selection
- risk management
- dynamic capital reallocation

The objective is not merely to identify profitable assets.

The objective is to understand how assets behave together.

---

# Guiding Principle

Owning multiple assets does not necessarily reduce risk.

If those assets consistently move together, they may represent a single underlying exposure.

True diversification comes from understanding relationships rather than simply increasing the number of positions.

# Fundamental Question

The Asset Correlation Model continually asks:

> How does the behavior of one asset relate to the behavior of another?

The answer provides context.

It does not directly authorize trades.

---

# Correlation Philosophy

Financial assets rarely exist in isolation.

Many respond to common influences including:

- investor sentiment
- macroeconomic events
- interest rates
- inflation
- monetary policy
- sector rotation
- geopolitical events
- liquidity conditions

Understanding these relationships allows OmniTrade to make more informed allocation decisions.

---

# Correlation Is Descriptive

Correlation describes observed historical relationships.

It does not prove:

- causation
- future behavior
- permanent relationships

Correlation is evidence.

Not certainty.

---

# Types of Correlation

Future versions may classify relationships including:

Positive Correlation

Assets generally move together.

Negative Correlation

Assets generally move in opposite directions.

Weak Correlation

Little consistent relationship.

Unstable Correlation

Relationship changes over time.

Unknown

Insufficient evidence.

---

# Correlation Is Dynamic

Relationships evolve.

Assets that historically moved together may later diverge.

Assets previously unrelated may become strongly connected.

Correlation should therefore be continuously reevaluated.

Historical relationships should never be assumed permanent.

---

# Opportunity Selection

Cross-Asset Opportunity Selection may incorporate correlation when comparing opportunities.

Example

Two opportunities possess identical expected value.

One significantly increases portfolio concentration.

The other improves diversification.

Future scoring models may prefer the opportunity producing healthier portfolio construction.

---

# Dynamic Capital Reallocation

Correlation may influence capital movement.

Example

Current Position

Bitcoin

New Opportunity

Ethereum

If both assets exhibit nearly identical behavior, reallocating capital may provide little additional benefit.

Conversely, reallocating into a less correlated opportunity may improve overall portfolio resilience.

---

# Risk Relationship

Risk evaluates the safety of individual opportunities.

Correlation evaluates collective exposure.

Example

Five individually acceptable positions may collectively represent excessive concentration if they consistently move together.

Risk and Correlation should therefore complement one another.

---

# Correlation Does Not Prohibit Investment

High correlation does not automatically prevent investment.

It provides context.

Portfolio Management determines whether correlated exposure remains acceptable.

Correlation informs decisions.

It does not replace them.

---

# Determinism

Given identical historical market data, the Asset Correlation Model should produce identical relationship assessments.

Replay should faithfully reproduce historical relationship analysis.

Determinism is essential for:

- replay
- auditing
- debugging
- AI review
- simulation

# Long-Term Vision

The Asset Correlation Model enables OmniTrade to understand financial markets as interconnected systems rather than collections of isolated assets.

Rather than asking:

"Should we own Bitcoin?"

or

"Should we own Ethereum?"

OmniTrade continually asks:

"How does adding this opportunity change the behavior, resilience, and expected performance of the portfolio as a whole?"

As the platform expands across providers, asset classes, and global financial markets, understanding relationships between assets becomes as important as understanding the assets themselves.

True financial intelligence requires understanding not only individual opportunities, but also how every opportunity interacts with every other opportunity already under management.
